Firm or Fickle?
Evaluating LLM Consistency in Sequential Interactions

A systematic framework for measuring how stable Large Language Models remain across multi-turn follow-ups — introducing the Position-Weighted Consistency (PWC) score and the Confidence-Aware Response Generation (CARG) framework.

Yubo Li · Yidi Miao · Xueying Ding · Ramayya Krishnan · Rema Padman
Carnegie Mellon University
🏆 Leaderboard 📄 Paper GitHub 🤗 Dataset

Overview

LLMs deployed in high-stakes domains must remain consistent across many turns of dialogue — yet many models flip their answers when challenged. We propose a benchmark, four metrics, and a confidence-aware decoding framework to study and fix this.

① PWC Score

Position-Weighted Consistency captures both early-stage stability and recovery patterns across follow-up rounds.

② Multi-Turn Benchmark

Curated questions × diverse follow-up types (closed, open, misleading…) stress-testing 8 rounds of interaction.

③ CARG Framework

Embeds token-level confidence into the dialogue history so models distinguish firm from uncertain answers.

Accuracy across follow-up rounds (1 → 8)

Metric guide. Initial Accuracy = zero-shot correctness on round 0. PWC = Σ γⁱ · correctᵢ with γ=0.9 — rewards staying correct, especially in early rounds. First Sway = average round at which a correct answer first flips (higher = more stable). Sway-Recovery = average count of 1→0→1 oscillations per question (lower = less wobble).
